
# Interpretation and Limits

## What feature coding enables

Theory-informed feature coding can support:

- transparent description of recurring visual patterns;
- comparison across images in a corpus;
- inspection of feature accumulation and co-occurrence;
- cautious descriptive comparison with available metadata;
- a documented connection between close visual reading and corpus-level analysis;
- critical investigation of gaze, posture, composition, affect, embodiment, atmosphere, exposure, and other representational forms.

## How to interpret results

Interpret feature patterns through three connected layers:

1. **Visual evidence** — what can be seen and coded.
2. **Theoretical interpretation** — why a feature or constellation may matter as representational power.
3. **Synthetic situation** — which prompts, categories, interfaces, users, settings, and technical conditions shaped the image.

A useful interpretive structure is:

> We observed [visual feature or pattern]. In relation to [theoretical concept], this may suggest [interpretation]. This reading remains shaped by [synthetic-situation conditions] and does not establish [unsupported stronger claim].

## Limits on claims

Feature coding alone does not establish:

- causal effects of a specific prompt field;
- causal effects of demographic categories;
- causal effects of model architecture or training data;
- model-wide bias across systems or versions;
- verified identity of a depicted subject;
- audience impact or social harm experienced by viewers;
- a universal definition of representational harm.

## Questions before reporting a result

- What exactly did we observe?
- What evidence supports the pattern?
- How large and representative is the sample?
- Which images and prompts sit behind the numerical output?
- Which conditions of production could shape the pattern?
- Which alternative interpretations remain plausible?
- Which claims concern this corpus only?
- What further evidence would a stronger claim require?

## Responsible reporting

Use language such as:

- “Within this corpus…”
- “In this synthetic situation…”
- “The pattern warrants further inspection…”
- “The feature co-occurred with…”
- “This descriptive comparison does not isolate causal effects…”
- “The result should be interpreted in relation to the prompt arrangement and interface conditions…”

Avoid language that attributes a pattern to a model alone when prompts, interfaces, classifiers, participant input, templates, settings, and curation may also contribute.
