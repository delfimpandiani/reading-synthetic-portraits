
# Ethics note for instructors and students

Synthetic portraits can contain racialised, gendered, sexualised, ableist, ageist, and otherwise harmful visual patterns. Classroom analysis should create conditions for careful discussion without asking students to disclose personal experiences or identities.

Use the following practices:

- establish discussion norms before viewing images;
- give content guidance where image samples contain sexualisation, stereotypes, bodily distortion, or discriminatory representation;
- allow students to opt out of direct engagement with particular images where feasible;
- use low-risk, approved, or instructor-held image samples;
- avoid public redistribution of participant-generated images and prompts;
- distinguish analysis of representational conventions from claims about depicted people;
- treat uncertainty and discomfort as information that may require methodological reflection.

The aim is to study how synthetic-image systems organise representation and power. The aim is not to classify people, verify identities, or reproduce harmful categories uncritically.
