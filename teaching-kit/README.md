# Teaching with the Synthetic Portrait Feature-Coding Protocol

This teaching kit supports individual and group exercises in theory-informed analysis of synthetic images. Students use critical theory as a sensitising orientation, inspect a synthetic-image corpus, develop observable feature categories, construct a codebook, test and revise it, and interpret descriptive patterns in relation to the synthetic situation that produced the images.

The aim is to learn how systematic visual analysis is constructed. Students do not need to reproduce the ctrl.alt.img codebook or arrive at one correct set of features. They should develop categories that are visually observable, theoretically meaningful, clearly defined, open to critique, and appropriate to their corpus.

## Choose an exercise

- **Same corpus, different theory:** compare how theoretical orientation shapes feature formation.
- **Same theory, different corpus:** compare how synthetic situations shape codebooks.
- **Shared codebook workshop:** move from individual observation to collaborative operationalisation.
- **Codebook critique:** revise an existing codebook and document the consequences.

## Student deliverables

Each exercise produces:

1. a synthetic-situation map;
2. a theoretical-orientation statement;
3. an individual seed-feature list;
4. a group codebook;
5. a pilot-coding decision log;
6. a short contextual interpretation.

## Core principle

Theory guides attention toward questions of representational power. Images supply the visual evidence, candidate features, and boundaries that make coding possible. The synthetic situation shapes both the images and the claims that can be made about them.
