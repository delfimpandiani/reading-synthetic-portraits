
# Facilitator guide

## Learning goals

By the end of the exercise, students should be able to:

1. describe a synthetic-image system as a situated sociotechnical arrangement;
2. use theory as a sensitising orientation for visual inspection;
3. distinguish visual observation from theoretical interpretation;
4. develop observable feature definitions collaboratively;
5. revise a codebook in response to ambiguity and disagreement;
6. produce cautious descriptive analysis;
7. articulate limits on their claims.

## Preparation

Prepare:

- a small image corpus that can be used ethically in class;
- any available contextual information about the corpus’s synthetic situation;
- a shared workspace for worksheets and codebooks;
- a selected theoretical reading or concept sheet for each group;
- a clear statement about respectful discussion of identity, bodies, race, gender, sexuality, disability, and appearance.

Use instructor-provided images or a locally held authorised corpus. Do not require students to upload participant-generated images or personal prompts to public systems.

## Suggested formats

| Format | Minimum time | Recommended time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Same corpus, different theory | 90 minutes | Two 2-hour sessions |
| Same theory, different synthetic situations | Two 2-hour sessions | Three sessions plus presentation |
| Shared codebook workshop | Two 2-hour sessions | Four sessions including pilot coding |
| Codebook critique and revision | 90 minutes | Two 2-hour sessions |

## Suggested corpus sizes

| Activity | Images per student or group |
|---|---:|
| Initial inspection | 12–20 |
| Group codebook formation | 20–40 |
| Pilot coding | 20–50 |
| Basic prevalence or co-occurrence analysis | 50–100 where feasible |

## Recommended facilitation sequence

### Session 1

1. Introduce synthetic situations and the protocol.
2. Explain the difference between observation, coding, and interpretation.
3. Complete the synthetic-situation worksheet.
4. Complete the theoretical-orientation worksheet.
5. Conduct individual image inspection and seed-feature generation.
6. Begin group discussion.

### Session 2

1. Complete the provisional codebook.
2. Pilot code a fresh image sample.
3. Record ambiguity, overlap, and disagreement.
4. Revise the codebook.
5. Generate one descriptive output.
6. Complete contextual interpretation and limits worksheet.

## Discussion prompts

- What did your theoretical orientation make easier to notice?
- Which concepts helped interpretation without becoming useful feature labels?
- Which candidate features were difficult to define visually?
- Where did group members disagree?
- What did the disagreement reveal about the image, concept, or definition?
- Which aspects of the synthetic situation might shape the patterns you observed?
- What would you need to know before making a stronger claim?

## Ethical guidance

Ask students to avoid treating bodies or identities as problems to diagnose. The exercise concerns representational conventions, system conditions, and visual patterns. It does not authorise speculation about a depicted person’s internal state, identity, morality, health, or intentions.

Encourage students to record uncertainty. Uncertainty is methodological information, especially for distorted images, ambiguous poses, incomplete bodies, and synthetic glitches.

## Assessment guidance

Assess the quality of process, definition, reflexivity, and revision. Avoid rewarding the number of features produced.
