# Student quick start

## Your task

Work individually and in a group to develop and test a feature-based codebook for a corpus of synthetic images.

## Eight steps

1. **Describe the synthetic situation.** Who or what produced the images? Which model, interface, prompt, categories, defaults, and settings shaped them?
2. **Choose a theoretical orientation.** Identify the concepts that guide what you attend to.
3. **Inspect the images.** Record visible patterns before making interpretive claims.
4. **Create seed features.** Turn recurring visible patterns into provisional feature names.
5. **Build a codebook.** Define each feature through inclusion, exclusion, and borderline cases.
6. **Pilot the codebook.** Apply it to a new group of images and document problems.
7. **Revise.** Clarify, merge, split, rename, or remove features.
8. **Interpret contextually.** Use theory and the synthetic situation to explain what the patterns may mean and which claims remain uncertain.

## Keep these distinctions clear

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Visual observation | “The subject’s eyes are directed downward.” |
| Feature | “Averted gaze.” |
| Interpretation | “This may position the subject as less available for reciprocal recognition.” |
| Contextual question | “Could the pose also reflect the prompt template, model style, or participant instruction?” |

## A good feature is

- visibly identifiable;
- defined clearly enough for another person to apply;
- distinguishable from nearby features;
- supported by examples and exclusions;
- open to revision after pilot coding.

## Avoid

- coding personality, intent, morality, or identity as though they were visible facts;
- treating a theoretical concept as an automatic code label;
- making causal claims from a small descriptive pattern;
- treating a generated image as an output of a model alone.
